# Racing-pigeon ring number formats: Central & Eastern Europe, Balkans, Turkey, former USSR

Method note: ~25 searches/fetches (2026-11-20). Strongest sources: the PZHGP flight-and-clock regulation of 11.11.2025 (official, Poland), Benzing MyPigeons guide (Slovakia), auction houses (Croatia), AviRings (Serbia, Slovenia), and the FCI "World Best Pigeon 2024" results PDF hosted by RPRA. Caveat on that WBP PDF: its extracted text layer has the name, ring and country columns out of alignment, so the ring strings are quoted verbatim but the country they sit next to in the extracted text is not reliable. Use the prefix, not the row, to place them. Several sources (PIPA prefix table, Pigero format help) could not be fetched (403 / DNS); their content is reported only from search snippets and marked as such.

## 1. Country codes on the ring and pedigree variants

### Takeaway
Confirmed in sources: PL (older RP), CZ (older CS), SK, HU, RO, BG, SRB (older "R.S."), CRO, SLO, BIH, TR, UA, RUS, LTU, GR. Pedigrees and results often drop the separators or the prefix entirely (Romanian, Ukrainian and some Polish entries appear with no country code). No source-backed code was found for North Macedonia, Montenegro, Albania, Moldova, Belarus, Latvia or Estonia.

### Cited Findings
- **Poland:** the regulation lists the country mark as "(PL, DV, CZ.....)" for current rings and "(RP, PL...)" for old district rings. RP is an old Polish mark. — [PZHGP Regulamin Lotowo-Zegarowy 11.11.2025, §4](https://pzhgp.pl/wp-content/uploads/REGULAMIN-LOTOWO-ZEGAROWY-11.11.2025.pdf)
- **Czech Republic:** the code is CZ ("CZ – Česká republika"). A commenter quotes an old ring "CS 037 75 3057" (Czechoslovakia). — [kotrlak.cz, Kroužek holuba](https://www.kotrlak.cz/clanky/zaletli-holubi_-hledani-holubu/cislo.krouzku.holuba.html)
- **Czech Republic:** TOPigeon club software tells the user to set the country ring code to "CZ" for Czechia. Search snippet only. — [postoveholuby.sk TOPigeon manual](https://www.postoveholuby.sk/storage/documents/topigeon-manual.pdf)
- **Slovakia:** SK. — [Benzing MyPigeons guide](https://mypigeons.benzing.live/en/guide/pigeons/)

## 2. Structure and order of parts (with real examples)

### Takeaway
There is no regional standard. Poland is PL-branch-YY-serial (the branch is printed with a leading zero on the ring). Czech is CZ-YY-club-serial on rings and in most pedigrees, though some sources reverse year and club. Slovakia (in Benzing) is SK-YYYY-5-digit-org-serial. Hungary is HU-YYYY-region-serial. Slovenia is SLO-YY-club-serial. Croatia is CRO-YY-5-digit serial. Serbia is SRB-YYYY-6-digit serial. Lithuania puts the club before the year (LTU-club-YY or YYYY-serial). Turkey is TR + YY + serial. Bulgaria is BG + 5-digit serial + YY (or BG YY-NNNNN).

### Cited Findings

**Poland (PZHGP), official.** The regulation of 11.11.2025, §4, sets a maximum of 15 characters. — [PZHGP 2025 regulation](https://pzhgp.pl/wp-content/uploads/REGULAMIN-LOTOWO-ZEGAROWY-11.11.2025.pdf)
- (a) The order is: country mark, branch (oddział) number, year "always two digits", serial number. Example: "PL-123-01-1234".
- (b) If it would exceed 15 characters, drop the dash between country and branch. Example: "PL123-01-12345".
- (c) Old rings with a district (okręg) number: country (RP, PL), district "always two digits", year (2 digits), serial. Example: "PL-01-96-123456".
- (d) Special rings such as DERBY: the special symbol follows the country. Example: "PL-DE-00-123456".
- (e) Rings with no branch or district: country, YY, serial. Example: "PL-11-1234567".

Polish examples:
- The 2005 version of the regulation writes the branch with a leading zero: "PL-0123-01-1234". — [PZHGP 2005 regulation page](https://pzhgp.pl/2018/05/04/2005-04-19-%E2%80%A2-regulamin-lotowo-zegarowy-poprawiony/)
- Toruń branch's "correct ring notation" page gives "PL-0326-19-1234". Search snippet. — [PZHGP Oddział 0326 Toruń](http://pzhgptorun.eu/czytaj/316/)
- Real auction lots: "PL-0384-25-1698" and "PL-03-25-3085". The latter looks like a short-form or district-style number; unexplained. — [aukcje.supergolab.pl](https://aukcje.supergolab.pl)
- Found-ring comments: "PL 0105 06 9752", "PL-O264-1O-15025" (letter O typed for zero), "PL 0329 60 11250" (the "60" is likely a misread). — [kotrlak.cz](https://www.kotrlak.cz/clanky/zaletli-holubi_-hledani-holubu/cislo.krouzku.holuba.html)
- In the WBP 2024 list: "PL-025-23-1668" (3-digit branch, no leading zero) and "23/0646" (no country, slash form). — [WBP 2024 Yearling](https://www.rpra.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/WBP-2024-YEARLING.pdf)

### Inferences
- Normalisation should key on: country alias, year normalised to 4 digits, optional org/club/region (keep leading zeros as a string), and serial (keep leading zeros as a string, e.g. CRO-23-09273).
- The year can appear before the org (CZ, SLO), after the org (PL, LTU, RUS), right after the country (SK, HU, SRB, CRO, TR), or at the end (RO in results, BG in WBP, SRB in WBP).
- Separators seen: "-", space, "/", none. Polish O/0 typos occur.
- For Czech, the 4-digit ZO beginning with 0 and the 2-digit year are distinguishable by length. CZ-YY-ZZZZ-N and CZ-ZZZZ-YY-N can be told apart heuristically: a 4-digit group starting with 0 is the club.
- For Poland, a 3-digit branch and the same branch written with a leading zero (123 / 0123) should normalise to the same value.
- Benzing and AviRings re-render rings into their own canonical COUNTRY-YYYY-ORG-SERIAL forms (SK-2022-01002-783, SLO-2024-101-009345, DV-2023-06687-547), which differ from what is stamped on the ring.

### Gaps
- No official ring specification was found for Czech (ČMSCHPH), Slovak (SZCHPH), Hungarian, Bulgarian, Serbian, Croatian, Slovenian, Turkish, Ukrainian, Russian or Lithuanian federations. Formats above are inferred from examples.
- Fewer than 3 verified examples were found for: Hungary (1), Bulgaria (1-2), Serbia (2), Turkey (3, snippet only), Ukraine (2, snippet), Russia (2), Lithuania (2), Greece (1, old), BIH (0).
- No examples were found at all for North Macedonia, Montenegro, Albania, Moldova, Belarus, Latvia or Estonia.
- Whether Slovak physical rings show the 5-digit org (01002) or a shorter club number is unverified.
- Whether Greek rings currently use GR or GRE is unverified.

## 3. Older formats and change years

### Takeaway
Only partial evidence was found. Poland had district (okręg) rings with a 2-digit district number (RP/PL) before the current branch rings, Czechoslovakia used CS, and Serbia earlier used "R.S.". None of the change years were documented.

### Cited Findings
- **Poland:** old rings carry the district number, "Stare obrączki rodowe z numerem Okręgów", example "PL-01-96-123456" (a 1996 ring). The country mark could be RP or PL. — [PZHGP 2025 regulation §4c](https://pzhgp.pl/wp-content/uploads/REGULAMIN-LOTOWO-ZEGAROWY-11.11.2025.pdf); [PZHGP 2015 regulation, branch 074](https://074.pzhgp.net/PZHGP/pliki/074/Dokumenty/20160215164827REGULAMINLOTOWOZEGAROWY21112015r.pdf)
- **Poland:** branch-number rings were already prescribed in the 2005 regulation ("PL-0123-01-1234", a 2001 ring). — [PZHGP 2005 regulation](https://pzhgp.pl/2018/05/04/2005-04-19-%E2%80%A2-regulamin-lotowo-zegarowy-poprawiony/)
- **Czechoslovakia:** "CS 037 75 3057" (the 75 written vertically, i.e. 1975), quoted by a commenter. — [kotrlak.cz](https://www.kotrlak.cz/clanky/zaletli-holubi_-hledani-holubu/cislo.krouzku.holuba.html)
- **Czech:** "CZ 994 05 070" (Litomyšl, unexplained 3-digit group). — [kotrlak.cz](https://www.kotrlak.cz/clanky/zaletli-holubi_-hledani-holubu/cislo.krouzku.holuba.html)
- **Serbia:** "R.S.-06". **Greece:** "GR-03". **Bulgaria:** "BG-90" plus a 2004 metal ring. All from an old collector forum, search snippet. — [Pigeonbasics forum](http://forum.pigeonbasics.com/topic/9208-some-of-my-rings/)
- **Ukraine:** older ring years were written as "-01-" (= 2001). Older rings were all-metal; newer ones have a metal core inside a plastic shell. — [aqua-svit.com.ua](https://aqua-svit.com.ua/koral/%D0%9A%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%B8%20%D0%B3%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%83%D0%B1%D1%96%D0%B2/)

### Inferences
- The Polish switch from district to branch rings happened before 2001. The 2005 regulation already shows a 2001 branch ring, and 1996 appears as a district-ring example. The exact year is not documented.
- The Czech switch from CS to CZ presumably followed the 1993 split. This is not documented in the sources found.

### Gaps
- No sources were found for YU (Yugoslavia) racing rings, USSR/СССР rings, or the year each successor state began issuing its own code.
- No source gives the year RP stopped being used in Poland.
- No sources were found for the older Romanian club formats mentioned in the brief.

## 4. Sex notation on pedigrees

### Takeaway
No federation source documents local sex letters for any country in scope. Results lists show only isolated suffixes (German "W", "/M").

### Cited Findings
- WBP 2024 shows the suffix " W" on a German ring ("DV 06210-23-1007 W", W = Weibchen) and "/M" on "398221/23/M". The second string sits in the Bulgaria area of the extracted text; row attribution is unreliable. — [WBP 2024 Yearling](https://www.rpra.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/WBP-2024-YEARLING.pdf)
- The PZHGP regulation's ring-notation section (§4) defines no sex marker; the 15-character limit covers the ring only. — [PZHGP 2025 regulation](https://pzhgp.pl/wp-content/uploads/REGULAMIN-LOTOWO-ZEGAROWY-11.11.2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- A parser should strip a trailing sex token after the ring (single letter or 1.0/0.1) before matching. The local vocabulary below is unverified and should be treated as candidates only:
  - Polish S/M?
  - Czech/Slovak "H/S" or "1,0/0,1"?
  - Romanian M/F
  - Russian/Ukrainian "С/К" or "♂/♀"

### Gaps
- No source was found for the sex abbreviations used on Polish, Czech, Slovak, Romanian, Bulgarian, ex-Yugoslav, Greek, Turkish, Russian, Ukrainian or Baltic pedigrees. This needs a targeted search of pedigree PDFs per country.

## 5. Ambiguities with other countries' codes

### Takeaway
Several real collisions exist: Polish "DE" means Derby, not Germany; "CS" means old Czechoslovakia; Croatia and Slovenia use CRO/SLO rather than ISO HR/SI; the country code can trail the number (Serbia) or be absent (Romania, Ukraine, some Polish entries); Benzing writes Germany as DV.

### Cited Findings
- In Poland, "PL-DE-00-123456" uses DE for a special DERBY ring, not Germany. Germany's ring mark is DV. — [PZHGP 2025 regulation §4d, §4a](https://pzhgp.pl/wp-content/uploads/REGULAMIN-LOTOWO-ZEGAROWY-11.11.2025.pdf)
- Serbia's code is printed as a suffix in FCI WBP results ("414415-23-SRB"). — [WBP 2024 Yearling](https://www.rpra.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/WBP-2024-YEARLING.pdf)
- Ukrainian found-pigeon records omit UA by design. — [pigeons.in.ua FAQ](https://pigeons.in.ua/Home/FAQ)
- Romanian results omit RO ("579175-2023"). — [WBP 2024 Yearling](https://www.rpra.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/WBP-2024-YEARLING.pdf)
- Lithuania writes LTU, while Latvia and Estonia remain unverified. — [LBSF istorija](http://balandziusportas.lt/istorija-2/lt/)

### Inferences
- Without a prefix, Romanian "NNNNNN-YYYY" and other 6-7 digit forms (e.g. NL "2023-9218140", South African "2014487-2023" in the same WBP list) cannot be told apart by pattern alone. Software should ask the user or use the loft's default country.
- "CS" was also the ISO code of Serbia and Montenegro (2003-2006). This is general knowledge, not confirmed in pigeon sources. A ring "CS …" from 2003-2006 is therefore ambiguous between Czechoslovakia (pre-1993) and Serbia & Montenegro, though Czechoslovak rings would carry pre-1993 years.
- "RS" (Serbia ISO), "RU", "SI" and "HR" may appear in software-generated pedigrees even though rings use SRB, RUS, SLO and CRO.
- Polish "PL-03-25-3085" (2-digit group before the year) collides with the old-district pattern "PL-01-96-123456". The year disambiguates.

### Gaps
- No source confirms the codes used by North Macedonia (MKD?), Montenegro (MNE?), Albania (AL/ALB?), Moldova (MD?), Belarus (BY/BLR?), Latvia (LV?) or Estonia (EST?).
- Without them it cannot be checked whether, for example, MD collides with anything or Belarus uses BY or BLR.
